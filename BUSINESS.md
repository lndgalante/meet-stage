# BetterMeets Business Brief

> Market and pricing hypotheses as of August 7, 2026. Validate them with paid
> customer behavior before treating them as facts.

## Executive view

BetterMeets can be a paid product, particularly for people whose credibility or
revenue depends on frequent live software demos. The strongest initial market is
not everyone who shares a screen. It is presenters who switch between several
apps during important calls and experience the cost of a broken flow repeatedly.

The best initial customer profile is:

- Sales engineers and solutions consultants at B2B software companies.
- Developer advocates and customer engineers.
- Founders conducting their own sales and product demos.
- Product engineers who demonstrate software several times per week.

Marketing teams are a plausible expansion market for launches, webinars, and
live product education. They are not the best first market because they often
need additional production features such as branded layouts, camera composition,
recording, and team workflows.

The product should be sold as a **control layer for flawless live product
demos**, not as a window switcher.

## Product promise

**Stay in flow. Look polished.**

Functional promise: **Share once. Switch freely.**

BetterMeets gives the presenter one clean Demo Stage to share in a meeting. The
presenter can then move between app windows from the compact controller or global
shortcuts without reopening the share picker or exposing the desktop.

The commercial value comes from four outcomes:

1. Preserve the presenter's flow and attention.
2. Prevent accidental exposure of unrelated or private content.
3. Make transitions feel deliberate and professional.
4. Reduce uncertainty about what the audience can currently see.

## The customer problem

Native meeting software allows a user to present a tab, a window, or an entire
screen, but the presenter must still manage the source and sharing workflow.
Changing content can introduce a picker, a pause, an incorrect source, accidental
desktop exposure, or confusion about what is live.

Google's Meet documentation reflects these constraints: feature availability
varies by sharing mode, window selection is limited to open and non-minimized
windows, and changing a presented tab has its own workflow. BetterMeets creates
one persistent output window above that fragmented workflow.

This problem becomes commercially meaningful when:

- The presenter gives multiple demos every week.
- A demo influences a sale, renewal, launch, or customer relationship.
- The story crosses a browser, terminal, IDE, slides, design tools, or native
  applications.
- Exposing another window or notification would be embarrassing or risky.
- Several presenters on a team need a repeatable demo setup.

### Recommended initial ICP

A macOS user at a B2B software company who:

- Runs at least three live product demos per week.
- Usually switches among at least three windows or applications.
- Presents to prospects, customers, partners, or a public audience.
- Has experienced an awkward share-picker pause or accidental exposure.
- Can purchase a professional utility directly or expense it with minimal
  approval.

### Buyer and user

The initial buyer and user can be the same person. This supports a self-serve
Mac utility model. The team buyer appears later:

| Stage | User | Likely buyer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual | Sales engineer, founder, developer advocate | The individual |
| Small team | Solutions engineering or developer relations team | Team lead or manager |
| Organization | Customer-facing technical teams | Sales engineering, enablement, or IT leadership |

## Jobs to be done

When I am demonstrating software live, help me:

- Set up one safe output before the call begins.
- Know exactly which window is visible to the audience.
- Switch to the next part of the story without stopping to operate the meeting.
- Keep sensitive, unfinished, or irrelevant content off the shared stage.
- Recover gracefully when a window closes, moves, or becomes unavailable.
- Reuse a familiar setup and shortcuts in the next demo.

## Positioning

### Category

Live demo control software for macOS.

### Positioning statement

For technical presenters who move across several apps during important calls,
BetterMeets is a live demo control layer that keeps one clean stage shared while
the presenter switches sources instantly. Unlike sharing individual windows
directly, BetterMeets preserves the meeting share, hides the desktop, confirms
what is actually live, and keeps important windows on predictable shortcuts.

### Suggested headline

**The control layer for flawless live product demos.**

### Suggested supporting copy

Share one clean Demo Stage in your meeting. Switch between apps instantly
without exposing your desktop or interrupting your presentation.

Compatibility claims such as “Works with Meet, Zoom, and Teams” should appear
only after each service has been tested in the release QA matrix.

### Message pillars

- **Stay in flow.** Move through the story without returning to a share picker.
- **Share intentionally.** Only the selected source reaches the Demo Stage.
- **Know what is live.** A source is marked live only after a complete frame is
  rendered.
- **Move predictably.** Use persistent shortcuts for important windows.
- **Look polished.** Preserve source geometry, resolution, and deliberate motion.

## Why customers would pay

BetterMeets is inexpensive relative to the value of an important demo. A sales
engineer does not need the app to produce revenue on its own; it needs to prevent
enough hesitation, confusion, or lost credibility to justify a modest utility
purchase.

The value calculation is strongest when:

- One saved enterprise demo is worth far more than the license.
- A presenter avoids several minutes of friction every week.
- The team wants consistent presentation behavior from every presenter.
- The product reduces anxiety enough that the presenter can focus on the buyer.

The adjacent Mac software market demonstrates that professionals already pay
for focused presentation and productivity tools. This is supporting evidence,
not proof of demand for BetterMeets:

- CleanShot sells a focused Mac capture utility for **$29 as a one-time
  purchase**, including one year of updates and an optional renewal.
- Ecamm's broader live production product lists team licenses from roughly
  **$310 to $384 per seat per year** for small teams.

BetterMeets is currently narrower than a production suite and should begin near
the professional utility end of this range.

## Pricing and packaging hypothesis

### Initial individual product

| Offer | Price | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Early customer price | $39 one time | Validate real willingness to pay with a limited cohort |
| Standard individual license | $59 one time | Clear, low-friction price for a focused professional utility |
| Optional updates renewal | $19–29 per year | Fund continued compatibility and improvements without disabling the owned version |

The initial license should include one year of updates. The purchased version
should continue working if the customer does not renew updates.

A recurring subscription is difficult to justify while BetterMeets is a local,
single-user utility. Avoid forcing one merely because the audience is
professional.

### Future team product

A team plan becomes credible after BetterMeets adds recurring organizational
value such as:

- Shared demo workspaces and approved source configurations.
- Brand assets, layouts, and reusable demo templates.
- Centralized billing, seat management, and license deployment.
- Managed shortcut or privacy policies.
- Team analytics that are respectful of presenter and customer privacy.
- Priority support and release compatibility guarantees.

An initial team hypothesis is **$99–149 per user per year**, but it should not be
launched until team-specific value exists and purchasing interviews validate the
range.

## Go-to-market recommendation

### Start narrow

Lead with sales engineers, solutions consultants, developer advocates, and
founders who demo technical products on macOS. A narrow message will resonate
more strongly than presenting BetterMeets as a general screen-sharing tool.

### Acquisition channels to test

- Short before-and-after demo videos on LinkedIn and X.
- Sales engineering and developer relations communities.
- Founder and indie Mac software communities.
- Direct outreach to people publishing live demo or webinar content.
- Partnerships with demo coaches, sales enablement consultants, and technical
  presentation educators.
- A referral offer for teams after an individual becomes an active user.

### Demonstration format

The product should sell itself visually in under 60 seconds:

1. Show the native workflow interrupting a live demo.
2. Share the BetterMeets Demo Stage once.
3. Switch among a browser, terminal, IDE, and slides using shortcuts.
4. Show that the desktop and unrelated windows never appear.
5. End with “Share once. Switch freely.”

## Validation plan

Do not validate the idea only by asking whether people like it. Validate payment
and repeated use in real calls.

### Cohort

Recruit 15 people who conduct at least three live software demos per week:

- 6 sales engineers or solutions consultants.
- 3 developer advocates or customer engineers.
- 3 founders conducting their own sales.
- 3 product engineers or product marketers who demo frequently.

### Process

1. Observe or reconstruct their current demo workflow.
2. Ask about the most recent share-picker mistake or awkward transition.
3. Install BetterMeets and get them to a successful first switch within two
   minutes.
4. Let them use it in real demos for 14 days.
5. Ask for **$39–49 to keep it** rather than asking a hypothetical pricing
   question.
6. Interview payers and non-payers separately.

### Strong early signals

- At least 5 of 15 qualified participants pay.
- The app is used in external customer or prospect calls, not only internal
  experiments.
- Paying participants are still using it after two weeks.
- Multiple participants ask about team purchasing or deployment.
- Users naturally rely on shortcuts and switch sources several times per demo.

### Interview questions

- How many live demos do you give in a typical week?
- How many apps or windows do you switch between?
- What went wrong in your most recent difficult demo?
- What do you do today to prevent accidental exposure?
- Who would approve or reimburse this purchase?
- What would make you stop trusting the product?
- Would you be comfortable using it in your next important customer call?
- Would you pay today to keep using the tested version?

## Product requirements for paid trust

The product will live or die on reliability. Before a broad paid launch:

1. Capture quality should be visually indistinguishable from direct window
   sharing under normal meeting conditions.
2. Source switching should be fast, deterministic, and truthful.
3. No previous frame, private window, desktop, controller, or title-bar chrome
   should leak during a transition or stop action.
4. Setup and permission onboarding should take less than two minutes.
5. Failure states should be obvious to the presenter and safe for the audience.
6. The packaged app should be properly signed, notarized, and updateable.
7. Supported meeting apps and macOS versions should have a repeatable QA matrix.

## Risks and responses

| Risk | Why it matters | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native sharing feels good enough | The free alternative is already installed | Target frequent multi-window presenters and demonstrate the interruption clearly |
| Capture quality is worse than direct sharing | Professional users will reject visible degradation | Treat output fidelity as a release-blocking requirement |
| A transition exposes the wrong content | One privacy failure can destroy trust | Default to safe empty output and show live state only after a confirmed frame |
| The product is seen as a small utility | Limits subscription and team pricing | Keep the initial purchase simple; earn expansion through workspaces and team controls |
| macOS-only narrows the market | Some teams standardize on Windows | Win a clear Mac niche first; measure lost demand before considering another platform |
| Meeting apps change capture behavior | Compatibility regressions damage confidence | Maintain a release QA matrix and communicate supported combinations |
| Marketing requires production features | The initial product may feel incomplete to that segment | Do not lead with general marketing; expand only after the core workflow is trusted |

## Revenue-aligned product roadmap

### Phase 1: Trusted individual utility

- Flawless capture fidelity and transitions.
- Stable shortcuts and accurate live state.
- Clear onboarding, permissions, and recovery.
- Signed and notarized distribution.
- Trial, license purchase, and update delivery.

### Phase 2: Repeatable demo workspaces

- Named sets of windows for different demos.
- Automatic restoration and unavailable-source guidance.
- Per-demo shortcuts and stage settings.
- Preflight checks for missing windows, unsafe content, and capture permission.
- Presenter notes or a lightweight run-of-show, if interviews support it.

### Phase 3: Team and brand value

- Shared configurations and templates.
- Branded stages, layouts, or camera composition.
- Centralized licenses and managed deployment.
- Team compatibility policies and support.

Avoid expanding into a full broadcasting studio unless customer evidence points
there. BetterMeets' initial advantage is speed, discretion, and focus.

## Metrics

### Activation

- Time from launch to first successful live source.
- Percentage of users who share the Demo Stage in a real meeting.
- Percentage who configure or use a global shortcut.

### Engagement

- Active demo days per week.
- Source switches per active demo.
- Number of recurring demo workspaces, if introduced.
- Retention after 14 and 30 days.

### Commercial

- Qualified trial-to-paid conversion.
- Refund rate.
- Individual-to-team expansion requests.
- Revenue per active presenter.
- Support incidents per 100 demos.

Do not collect window titles, captured content, participant data, or customer
meeting details merely to populate analytics. Privacy should reinforce the
product promise.

## Near-term decisions

1. Confirm sales engineers and adjacent technical presenters as the initial ICP.
2. Test a $39 early-customer purchase instead of a free open beta.
3. Keep the standard individual product near $59 one time with optional paid
   updates.
4. Make capture fidelity, privacy, and reliability release gates.
5. Build a 60-second before-and-after sales demo and a focused landing page.
6. Interview team buyers before building administrative features.
